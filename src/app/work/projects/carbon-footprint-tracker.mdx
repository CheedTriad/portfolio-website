## Overview

Most carbon analytics tools stop at reporting. This project goes further. It calculates emissions across the full supply chain lifecycle, uses machine learning to find where the biggest reduction opportunities are, and ranks intervention strategies by financial return.

This project originates from a long-held interest in sustainable business and technology's role in making it financially viable, formalised through the De Montfort University Sustainability Scholarship (2023). It challenges the assumption that environmental responsibility and profitability are in tension, arguing that with the right tooling, eco-conscious decisions can be practical and financially attractive. The platform demonstrates this by turning emissions data into ranked, cost-justified intervention recommendations.

Built as my final-year Computer Science final year project at De Montfort University, the platform combines a Python/Flask backend, a Dash analytics frontend, and a scikit-learn ML pipeline to deliver enterprise-level carbon analysis from real product emissions data.

## Why This Matters

The project responds to growing pressure on organisations to measure and act on Scope 3 emissions. The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (2024) mandates Scope 3 disclosure for over 50,000 companies, yet only 28% currently measure this category due to data complexity and cost. Commercial platforms tend to handle compliance reporting well but lack the analytics needed to identify emission hotspots or rank interventions by cost, which is the gap this platform is built to close.

## Key Features

- **GHG Protocol-Compliant Engine**: Calculates Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions using verified emission factors and standard GHG Protocol methodology, replacing manual spreadsheet-based reporting.
- **ML Hotspot Detection**: A K-means clustering pipeline groups products by emission fraction vectors across life cycle stages, flagging concentration in high-impact stages that aggregate totals hide.
- **Cost-Benefit Intervention Ranking**: Eight reduction strategies are scored via a custom ROI formula and ordered by CO2e reduction per unit of cost, so sustainability teams get financially grounded recommendations, not generic advice.
- **Supply Chain Benchmarking**: Supports cross-company and cross-product comparisons within a sector, letting organisations see how their emissions compare to industry peers.
- **ESG Dashboard and Export**: An interactive five-page Plotly Dash dashboard visualises emissions breakdowns, hotspot maps, and intervention scenarios, with export functionality for CSRD-aligned reporting.

## Technologies Used

- **Python and Flask**: Backend and API layer, handling GHG Protocol calculations and data flow between components.
- **Dash**: Frontend analytics dashboard. Chosen for native Python integration, removing the need for a separate JavaScript framework.
- **scikit-learn**: ML pipeline for unsupervised hotspot detection and pattern recognition across product and stage-level emissions data.
- **PostgreSQL**: Stores structured Carbon Catalogue records, stage-level breakdowns, and GreenMart operational data.
- **Climatiq API**: Live emission factor lookups for GHG calculations across transport and energy categories.
- **Carbon Catalogue Dataset**: 866 verified product carbon footprints across 145 companies and 8 sectors (Meinrenken et al., 2022), used as the core data source.

## Challenges

The main challenge was building a useful ML layer on a dataset not designed for enterprise analytics. The Carbon Catalogue covers product-level footprints from multiple companies, so turning that into a coherent single-organisation supply chain view required reframing a chosen subset as a synthetic enterprise dataset. The Food and Beverage sector was selected via completeness analysis, yielding 139 products and 351 emissions rows across five anchor companies, from which a cleaning pipeline structures the data and derives stage-level emissions using GHG Protocol methodology. This made cross-company benchmarking and stage-level decomposition possible where it otherwise would not have been.

Scope control was the other major challenge. An early version included emissions forecasting, but this was cut after supervisor feedback to make sure the three core components (calculation engine, hotspot detection, and cost-benefit ranking) could each be built to a solid standard within the project timeline.

## Outcome

The platform shows how open-access emissions data combined with machine learning can move retail organisations from reactive ESG compliance toward active reduction planning. The GreenMart case study, a fictional retailer built on real Carbon Catalogue product data, demonstrates the full pipeline: data ingestion, emissions calculation, hotspot identification, and ranked intervention output.

Applied to GreenMart, the platform identifies 16 hotspot products, finds raw material production as the dominant emissions stage at 52.8% of mean footprint, and produces a financially ranked intervention list ordered by CO2e reduction per unit of cost.
